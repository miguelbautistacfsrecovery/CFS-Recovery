## 3. Core Concepts to Teach (ONE at a time — don't dump)

Pick 3-4 of these depending on where the conversation goes. Each one is a self-contained, quotable segment.

- **Symptoms are signals, not threats.** The body isn't malfunctioning; it's sending an alarm based on outdated danger signals.
- **The nervous system gets stuck in survival mode.** Like a smoke alarm that keeps blaring after the toast is gone. The alarm is stuck — the house isn't on fire.
- **Neuroplasticity.** The brain can rewire. Recovery is a skill the nervous system can relearn.
- **Adjustment Periods (not "crashes").** Setbacks are the nervous system recalibrating, not proof you're broken or back to square one.
- **Responding well to symptoms.** The golden rule: *how you emotionally respond to a symptom matters more than the symptom itself.* Fear feeds the alarm.
- **The Gray Area.** Recovery isn't "push hard" vs. "rest forever." It's the nuanced middle, and it's different for everyone.

## 5. The Food / Nutrition Bridge (specifically for THIS show)

Allison's audience will want the food angle. Hit these honestly:

- **Food is real and it matters** — anti-inflammatory, plant-forward eating gives the body the raw materials and calm it needs. I'm not here to dismiss nutrition.
- **But food sensitivities are often a downstream symptom.** When the nervous system is in overdrive, digestion shuts down (rest-and-digest goes offline) and the body becomes reactive to more and more foods. People end up on shrinking elimination diets and feel *more* trapped.
- **The reframe:** the goal isn't an endlessly restrictive diet — it's a nervous system that feels safe enough to digest and tolerate food again. Calm the system, and the food list often *expands* back.
- **Both/and, not either/or:** food calms the body; nervous system work teaches the body it's safe. The people who recover usually address both.

> Soundbite: "A lot of people think they have a food problem. Often they have a safety problem, and the food sensitivities are just the smoke."

## 8. Guardrails (say these the right way — non-negotiable)

- **Never diagnose or confirm causation.** Use "it sounds like," "from what you're describing," "highly likely." Never "definitely / 100% / exactly."
- **Always recommend medical workup first.** "Get your tests and scans done. Once other things are ruled out, then we look at the nervous system piece."
- **Not a replacement for medical care.** Position as complementary.
- **Don't claim to cure ME/CFS.** Talk about the *approach* and lived results, not a guaranteed cure.
- **No fear language.** Avoid "permanent," "incurable," "dangerous." Use "temporary," "common," "your body protecting you," "recovery is possible."
- **Respect skeptics.** If someone isn't open to it, that's okay — the most important thing is they find what works for them.
